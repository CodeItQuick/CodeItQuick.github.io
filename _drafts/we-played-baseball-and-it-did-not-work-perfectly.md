In response to the classic article by Ron Jefferies: https://ronjeffries.com/xprog/articles/jatbaseball/

A quick chatgpt query tells me that baseball peaked in 1956. This exact year is the peak of the “golden peak” years from 
1947 to 1960. It was near the centre of American popular culture; 34% of Americans named baseball as their favourite sport, 
while only 21% named football. Recent variations of baseball is just in constant decline, and we aren’t really playing 
baseball unless it is exactly 1956.

First of all, the same nine players had to play the whole game. There were no designated hitters, pinch hitters, or any 
of that other nonsense. The game has just been built to scale to a team size and degree that is totally unnecessary. It’s 
stupidly inefficient to have any other number of players in the game.

With only nine players, every substitution had a cost. We didn’t need anymore players; if a player left the game the team 
just played down a man. If we lost a pitcher we just got one of the outfielders to throw the ball. If we lost the catcher 
we’d throw in the third baseman. If we lost an outfielder we would sub in our short stop. There was no substitute for 
constant problem solving through injuries.

The “equipment” required by modern baseball is overpriced, outrageous, and cruel. An entire team in 1956 could be equipped 
for less than $100, while modern teams require thousands of dollars of equipment to play a single game. Bats should be 
made entirely of white ash, not the traditional maple used today. There’s no need for optimizing balance and swing like 
they do today. The gloves of old didn’t need those deeper pockets, different webs, padding, and stronger lacing. The 
classic gloves of 1956 worked perfectly. Catchers lug around today like an armadillo, while only requiring the classic 
1956 protection in the first place.

In terms of the field, mounds have gotten much too tall today at 18 inches, which only required 15 inches in the ideal 
classic baseball. There’s no need to waste that extra bit of dirt. Synthetic turfs are just an overuse of modern day 
polymers, where we could just use natural grass. Furthermore, the outfield shape does not need the deliberate design of 
modern times. Much better to add character to every field. Lastly, an open air field and basic drainage is all we need. 
Retractable roofs and modern irrigation are a waste of precious resources and much too rigid an approach.

The thing that concerns me the most about baseball however, even with all these classic rulesets, is the game is still not perfectly played.
